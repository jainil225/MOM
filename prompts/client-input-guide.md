# Getting the best Minutes of Conversation — what to give us

AiX Track prepares Minutes of Conversation (MOC) from your meeting recording. The
quality of the minutes depends almost entirely on two things: how clearly the
conversation was captured, and how much context we are given about it.

This note sets out what to provide. It takes two minutes to follow and makes a
visible difference to the output.

---

## 1. The recording

The minutes can only record what can be heard. Most quality problems trace back
to the audio, not the analysis.

**Please do**

- Record from one device placed near the people speaking, not at the far end of
  the table.
- Use a headset or a conference microphone for online meetings. Laptop
  speakerphone in a large room is the most common cause of lost content.
- Ask people to speak one at a time. Long stretches of people talking over each
  other cannot be reliably separated.
- Open with a round of introductions — name and role. This lets us attribute
  points to the right person throughout.
- Say figures clearly and once: "eighteen per cent", "twelve lakh forty-five
  thousand", "by the twentieth of November".

**Please avoid**

- Holding the meeting on speakerphone with the device across the room.
- Recording only part of the meeting and expecting full minutes.
- Background music, television or a noisy corridor.

---

## 2. Tell us who was in the room

This is the single most useful thing you can give us.

Send a short list before or with the recording:

| Name (correct spelling) | Role / designation | Organisation |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

Why it matters: speech recognition frequently spells the same Indian name three
different ways in one meeting. Without a list, the same person can appear as
three people in the attendee list and in the action items. With a list, names are
correct and consistent every time.

If the meeting is one of a recurring series, give us the list once and we will
apply it to every future meeting in that series.

---

## 3. Tell us what the meeting was about

A line or two is enough, but it changes the result:

- The purpose of the meeting — review, decision, planning, audit, negotiation.
- The entity, client, project or engagement it relates to.
- Any agenda, if one was circulated.
- Terms, abbreviations and internal shorthand we would not otherwise know —
  project code names, internal system names, client short names.
- Anything specific you want watched closely: a particular figure, a deadline, a
  disputed point.

If you have a prior set of minutes for the same series, send it. We can then
carry forward what was still open and show what has moved since.

---

## 4. What you will receive

Each MOC is produced as a draft and includes, where the meeting supports it:

- Meeting details — title, date, time, venue or platform, attendees
- An executive summary
- Agenda or topics covered
- Topic-by-topic discussion, with the figures, dates and documents reported, and
  the position each participant took
- A decision log — what was decided, by whom, and on what basis
- An action tracker — task, owner, due date and priority
- Open items and confirmations still to be resolved
- Risks, issues, blockers and dependencies
- Points requiring your confirmation before the minutes are treated as final

Meetings held in Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam,
Bengali or Punjabi — or a mix with English — are prepared in formal English, with
names of people, companies and places kept as spoken.

---

## 5. What we will not do

So that the minutes remain reliable:

- Nothing is invented. Where something was not stated, the minutes say
  "Not recorded" or "Not discussed" rather than filling the gap.
- Where two figures conflict in the recording, both are recorded and the conflict
  is flagged, rather than one being chosen silently.
- Only agreed actions appear in the action tracker. Suggestions and requests that
  were not accepted are kept separate as open items.
- The minutes are issued as a **draft**. Points listed under "Confirmation
  required" should be resolved by the participants before the minutes are
  approved or circulated as the final record.
- The minutes are a record of the conversation, not professional advice. Any
  legal, tax, accounting or regulatory position mentioned in a meeting should be
  confirmed with the responsible professional before it is relied upon.

---

## 6. Consent

Please make sure everyone in the conversation knows it is being recorded and will
be processed to prepare the minutes. Obtaining that consent is the
responsibility of the person recording the meeting.

---

## Quick checklist before you send a recording

- [ ] Audio recorded close to the speakers, one device
- [ ] Attendee list with correct name spellings and roles
- [ ] One or two lines on the purpose and the entity or project
- [ ] Agenda, if one exists
- [ ] Any abbreviations or internal terms we should know
- [ ] Previous minutes, if this is a recurring meeting
- [ ] Anything specific you want watched closely
